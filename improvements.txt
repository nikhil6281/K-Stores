4.Your database is almost certainly open. The owner updates orders and products without any Firebase login. For that to work, the rules must let anyone write. Anyone could therefore change prices, delete products, or edit orders.
5.Every visitor downloads every customer's order. The page subscribes to the whole orders list, so other people's names, phone numbers and addresses reach every browser and are cached in localStorage. This is a privacy risk under India's DPDP Act.
6.Every cart tap rewrites the shared catalog. save() writes the visitor's local product list to Firebase, so a stale or offline visitor can overwrite your prices and stock.
7.Settings don't reach customers. Delivery fee, free-delivery limit and WhatsApp number are saved only on the owner's device.
8.Failed orders look successful. If the Firebase write fails, the customer still sees "Order Placed 🎉" and you never get the order.
9.Switching payment method wipes the form. setPay() redraws checkout, so the name, phone and address the customer typed are lost.
10.The owner's packing checkboxes reset whenever a new order arrives.
